Violet Sorrengail is the protagonist of *Fourth Wing* and the Empyrean series: a scribe trained to read, forced into the Riders Quadrant to fly. She bonds two dragons, Tairn and Andarna — unprecedented at Basgiath — and manifests lightning wielding and dream walking. This page covers her history, her signets, her dragons and her story across all three books.

> **Spoiler notice:** this page covers *Fourth Wing*, *Iron Flame* and *Onyx Storm* in full, including the ending of *Onyx Storm*. Book-by-book sections are ordered so you can stop at the book you have finished.

## Before the Riders Quadrant

Violet was born in July 613 AU, the third child of General Lilith Sorrengail and Lieutenant Colonel Asher Sorrengail, after her older siblings Brennan and Mira. A fever her mother caught during pregnancy left Violet with a connective tissue disorder the books never name — one that makes her joints subluxate, her skin bruise, and her bones break under pressure that would leave another cadet untouched.

She was raised a scribe, and by nearly every account the best mind of her generation. Her father filled her childhood with books and taught her to verify her sources and chase first-hand accounts — the habit that later saves her life more than once. Professor Kaori tells her plainly that she outpaces both Brennan and Mira intellectually, and may have more compassion than either.

Two details from early life matter later. At two, her father carried her to the isle of Unnbriel to dedicate her to Dunne, the goddess of war; the dedication was never completed. An oracle declared that Violet held more than one possible destiny and could not be bound to a single path. And her hair — brown to the shoulder, then fading to a metallic silver — follows the same pattern no matter how often she cuts it. Both facts keep mattering.

At eighteen she entered her first serious relationship, with Prince Halden Tauri. It ended after seven months. [FACT: 关系结束的具体经过——empyreanriders 有细节，建议对原著再写]

The year before *Fourth Wing* began, her father died of heart failure. Before Conscription Day, her mother refused to let another child enter the Scribe Quadrant, and Violet retrained for the Riders' entrance assessment in a single spring, placing in the upper quarter for speed and agility.

## Signet One: Lightning Wielding

Violet's primary signet, channelled through Tairn, is **lightning wielding**. It was one of the last first-year signets to surface, and it surfaced badly: during a War Games engagement her fury outran her restraint and she struck Jack Barlowe hard enough to bring down half a mountainside.

Felix Gerault's explanation is the one worth keeping — her power is raw energy that takes the shape of lightning because that is the form she instinctively reaches for. It responds to emotion more than to command, which is why basic targeting took sustained training and why it stays dangerous when Violet is not calm.

## Signet Two: Dream Walking

Violet's second signet, channelled through Andarna, lets her enter and move through a sleeping person's dreamscape. It belongs to the **inntinnsic** family — the signets that work directly on other minds — and is regarded as especially dangerous because a sleeping person cannot raise shields against it.

It showed its first signs in *Iron Flame* and became unmistakable in *Onyx Storm*. She has walked through both Xaden's dreams and Maren's.

## Her Dragons: Tairn and Andarna

**Tairneanach** — Tairn — is a black morningstartail and one of the most powerful dragons at Basgiath. He calls Violet "Silver One", calls her stubborn and worthy in the same breath, and means both. He is bonded to Sgaeyl, which ties him permanently to Xaden's orbit even across distance.

**Andarnaurram** — Andarna — is the dragon that makes Violet's story unprecedented. She bonds Violet as a small golden feathertail at Threshing, spends much of the series unable to fly, and turns out to belong to a seventh dragon breed previously thought not to exist.

Violet carries a relic on her back from the two bonds: a black dragon in full spread from shoulder to shoulder, with a golden silhouette at its centre.

## Personality and Weakness

Violet identifies as a scholar first and a rider second, and that ordering is the whole character. She reads the Codex for loopholes, adapts obstacles to her body instead of forcing herself through them, and treats mercy as a tactic rather than a luxury — targeting joints, using non-fatal poisons, refusing the finishing blow even when Tairn suggests it.

She is also stubborn in a way the dragons notice. Tairn calls her stubborn; she argues with her mother, her wingleader and her own dragons. Across the three books the anxious first-year who questioned her own legitimacy becomes a second-year who can absorb the news that she may be a form of inntinnsic herself without flinching.

Her weaknesses are stated in the books plainly enough to quote: the disorder that makes her body fragile, and the people she loves. Varrish names the second as her exploitable vulnerability, and he is right.
